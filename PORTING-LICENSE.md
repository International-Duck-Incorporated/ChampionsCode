To the extent of their respective rights, each rights holder grants IDI a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to:

- Access, inspect, audit, and analyze the Covered Modules.
- Port the Covered Modules to other Minecraft versions, mod loaders, platforms, APIs, and compatible software environments.

- Resolve compatibility issues, replace APIs, update dependencies, and restructure implementations.

These permissions apply to both source-code and binary forms to the extent permitted by the relevant rights holder's rights.

## 3. PERMITTED FUNCTIONALITY AND TECHNICAL SCOPE

The authorization may cover modules with functionality that is experimental, intrusive, exploit-oriented, disruptive, or capable of significantly changing normal software or gameplay behavior.

Where expressly included in Schedule A and authorized by the relevant rights holder, the permitted porting scope may include:

- Proofs of concept and security-testing modules.
- Vulnerability demonstrations and exploit-testing functionality.
- Modules capable of remote code execution (RCE).
- Modules that alter or disrupt expected game behavior.
- Game-breaking mechanics and substantial gameplay modifications.
- Automation of in-game actions and interactions.
- Chat-processing modifications and filter-related functionality.
- Protocol, networking, compatibility, and client-server behavior modifications.
- Features that change, bypass, disable, or replace normal application checks.
- Experimental modules that may cause crashes, instability, data loss, or unexpected behavior.

The presence of such functionality does not automatically exclude a module from the porting scope.

IDI may preserve, adapt, remove, disable, or replace individual features as required by the porting objectives, applicable law, technical requirements, and any feature-specific restrictions agreed to in writing.

This section authorizes the development and porting of the specified functionality. It does not grant permission to access third-party systems without authorization, execute code on another person's device without appropriate consent, or use vulnerabilities against systems outside an authorized scope.
